What Pittsburgh Landlords Actually Inspect

Pennsylvania landlords have 30 days after you vacate to return your deposit or provide an itemized list of deductions. The most common deductions we see:

1. Oven and Stovetop

This is the single most common deduction. Baked-on grease inside the oven and burner drip pans are almost always flagged. A standard wipe-down won't cut it — the oven needs to be degreased inside, including the door glass, racks, and the broiler drawer.

2. Refrigerator Interior

Landlords pull out the vegetable drawers and check the back walls. Any spills, odors, or residue will be noted. The coil area underneath also gets checked in many properties.

3. Bathroom Grout and Caulk

Pink or black mold in grout lines and around the tub caulk is a common deduction. If the caulk is moldy, it often needs to be replaced — something a cleaning alone won't fix, but a thorough scrub can prevent if caught early.

4. Walls and Baseboards

Scuff marks, crayon, and food splatter on walls are deductible. Landlords also check baseboards, which accumulate dust and grime that most tenants never think to clean.

5. Blinds and Window Tracks

Dusty blinds and debris-filled window tracks are easy to miss and easy to deduct. Each slat needs to be wiped, and the tracks need to be vacuumed and wiped out.

6. Carpet Condition

If you have carpet, it needs to be vacuumed thoroughly and spot-treated for stains. Many landlords require professional carpet cleaning regardless — check your lease.

7. Light Fixtures and Ceiling Fans

Dead bugs in light fixtures and dusty ceiling fan blades are a surprisingly common deduction. They're also easy to miss when you're focused on bigger tasks.

The Rooms Most Tenants Rush Through

Laundry area: The lint trap, drum interior, and the area behind the washer and dryer are almost always skipped. Landlords check them.

Closets: Closet floors, shelves, and the tops of closet shelves collect dust and debris. Don't forget the closet light fixtures.

Entryway: Scuff marks on the front door interior, the door frame, and the floor near the entrance are common deductions.

Garage or storage unit: If your rental includes a garage, it needs to be swept and cleared of any oil stains or debris.

What to Do Before the Cleaners Arrive

To get the most out of a professional move-out clean:

  1. Remove all personal belongings — cleaners can't clean around boxes and furniture
  2. Patch nail holes — cleaning crews don't do drywall repair; handle this before they arrive
  3. Note any pre-existing damage — document anything that was damaged before you moved in
  4. Be present for the walkthrough — or have someone available to let the team in

Timing Your Move-Out Clean

Schedule your clean after all your belongings are out and before the landlord's walkthrough. Ideally, you want 24–48 hours between the clean and the inspection so you can address anything that gets flagged.
